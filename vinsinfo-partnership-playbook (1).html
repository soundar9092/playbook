THE TWO-ENGINE PARTNERSHIP FRAMEWORK
01 · The framework

Two engines, one delivery core

Some partners pay us. Some partners send or receive work from us. Vinsinfo runs both at once — here's exactly how each one works.

The Two-Engine Partnership Framework (TEPF)

Definition: Vinsinfo's operating model for classifying, evaluating, and running every partner relationship — organised around two directions in which value moves. Engine A covers partners who pay Vinsinfo. Engine B covers partners Vinsinfo delivers with, or refers work to and from.

In plain words: before doing anything with a new partner, work out which direction the money and the work are actually moving in. That single question decides everything else in this playbook — which rules apply, what gets signed, and who needs to sign off.

Engine A

Platform Partners

SaaS, cloud and ecosystem tools pay Vinsinfo commission for the customers we bring them.

VinsinfoDelivery
Engine B

Channel Partners

Agencies and specialists exchange work with Vinsinfo — in either direction.

Looking for where these partners come from in the first place? See Section 11 · Partner Sourcing for the full procedure.

Engine A — Platform & Ecosystem Partnerships

Vinsinfo enrols as an official partner inside a platform's own programme — for example, a commerce platform, a cloud provider, or a payments tool. We were already going to use these tools to build client work; the difference is that once we're a registered partner, the platform pays us for driving customers to them.

Platform programme→Vinsinfo registers client→We deliver the build→Platform pays commission
Who owns the client

Vinsinfo — this is our own client relationship, we're simply also earning a second revenue stream from the platform underneath it.

Who sets the commission

The platform. Every programme (e.g. a commerce platform, a cloud provider, a payments tool) publishes its own rate — Vinsinfo doesn't negotiate it, only maximises it by registering correctly and advancing tiers.

What triggers payment

Usually the client going live and staying active on the platform — varies per programme, tracked per partner ID.

Example

We build a store on a SaaS platform, host it with a cloud provider, and wire up an ecosystem tool for payments (e.g. Shopify, AWS, Razorpay) — that's three separate Engine A commissions on one project.

Worked example

An exporter approaches Vinsinfo to set up an online store. We build it on a SaaS platform (e.g. Shopify), host the backend with a cloud provider (e.g. AWS), and integrate an ecosystem payment tool (e.g. Razorpay). Vinsinfo invoices the client directly for the build — and separately earns three ongoing Engine A commissions, one from each registered platform partnership, for as long as the client stays active on each one.

Engine B — Channel & Delivery Partnerships

Engine B runs in two directions. Inbound: another agency sends Vinsinfo client work to deliver. Outbound: Vinsinfo has a lead outside its own service scope — SEO, for example — and hands it to a partnered specialist instead of turning it away.

Inbound→Partner sends lead or scope→Vinsinfo delivers or bills
Outbound→Vinsinfo has an out-of-scope lead (e.g. SEO)→Redirected to a partnered specialist→Vinsinfo earns commission
Who owns the client

Whichever side actually delivers the work. Referral is the exception — ownership can stay with the introducer's brand depending on the deal.

Who sets the commission

Leadership, per agreement — referral runs 10–20%, decided and locked in at the time the partnership is signed, not a single fixed number across all partners.

Four models inside Engine B

Referral, White-label, Staff Augmentation, and Implementation Support — see Section 03 for how to tell them apart.

Example

An SEO enquiry lands in Vinsinfo's inbox. We don't do SEO — so we route it to a partnered SEO agency and earn 10–20% of that deal, as agreed with Leadership when the partnership was set up.

Worked example — outbound referral

An existing Vinsinfo client asks if we can also improve their search engine rankings. SEO is outside our scope, so we introduce them to a partnered SEO agency, under a referral agreement signed with Leadership at an agreed rate within our 10–20% range. The client signs a retainer with that agency. Vinsinfo earns its agreed share every month, for as long as that retainer runs — tracked in our CRM against the partner's record, paid out quarterly.

How do we co-sell with a partner?
+
Co-selling is a joint pitch, not a handoff — both sides show up to the same conversation with the same client. It applies whenever a live opportunity involves both Vinsinfo and a partner (an Engine A platform team helping us close a build, or an Engine B agency bringing us into their client pitch).
Step 1
Register the opportunity

Whoever spots it first logs it in CRM against the partner's record before the first joint call — this is what makes it a co-sell, not a referral.

Step 2
Agree who leads

One side leads the pitch and owns the deck; the other supports with technical or platform-specific credibility. Decided before the call, not during it.

Step 3
Joint pitch

Both sides join the client conversation. Vinsinfo brings delivery credibility; the partner brings their platform, brand, or client relationship.

Step 4
Close & attribute

Deal value and each side's share is confirmed against the CRM record from Step 1 — not renegotiated after the fact.

Who owns the opportunity, exactly?
+
One rule covers every model: whoever is logged as the CRM owner at the moment an opportunity is created stays the owner, regardless of who does more of the talking afterwards. For Engine A, that's almost always Vinsinfo — we own the client relationship even though the platform is also paid. For Engine B, ownership follows the model: referral ownership can sit with either side per the signed agreement, white-label and implementation ownership sits with whoever holds the client-facing brand, and staff-aug ownership always stays with the hiring partner. If it's ever unclear which model applies, run the Section 02 classifier again before assuming.
How do we plan for new partnerships?
+
Partnership sourcing runs on a quarterly cadence, not ad hoc. Each quarter: Leadership sets which category needs the most attention; the Partnerships Owner builds a shortlist from inbound interest, referrals, and direct outreach; each candidate runs through the Section 06 scorer before a conversation goes further than an intro call; and the resulting pipeline is reviewed with Leadership monthly alongside the revenue report, so new-partner effort stays tied to actual gaps rather than whoever reached out first.
08 · Keeping score

Governance, reporting & QBRs

The recurring cadence that keeps every partnership honest — from a single centralized system of record, reviewed on a fixed rhythm.

One centralized partner-management tool

Every partner — Engine A or Engine B, sourced last week or five years standing — lives in a single CRM instance. That record is the one source of truth this entire playbook refers back to: it's where opportunities are registered, where attribution is decided, where commission is tracked, and where QBR notes are logged. No partner data lives in a personal inbox, a spreadsheet on someone's laptop, or a WhatsApp thread. If it isn't in the centralized tool, it didn't happen for reporting purposes.

Monthly performance reporting

The Partnerships Owner circulates one combined report in the first week of every month — engine, category, and per-partner commission reconciled against actual bank credits and platform statements. See Section 07 for the tracking view.

Quarterly Business Reviews

Every partner above referral-only status gets a QBR each quarter — see the cadence below. Referral-only partners get a lighter check-in instead of a full QBR.

What a QBR actually covers

1
Performance against plan

Actual leads, revenue, and delivery volume versus what was expected when the partnership was scored and signed.

2
Pipeline health

What's currently registered in CRM for this partner, and its likelihood of closing this quarter.

3
Relationship & escalations

Any friction, attribution disputes, or quality issues raised since the last QBR — and whether they were resolved.

4
Next-quarter commitments

What each side commits to before the next QBR — logged in CRM against the partner's record, not left as a verbal agreement.

Attendees & cadence

Partnerships Owner and the named Vinsinfo delivery lead attend every QBR; the partner's own point of contact attends from their side. Leadership joins for any partner scoring "Green light" on the Section 06 scorer, or any partner under a Leadership review per Section 07. QBRs are scheduled a quarter in advance and are not cancelled without a make-up date already on the calendar.

09 · Ending things cleanly

Exit criteria

Every partnership this playbook helps start also needs a defined way to end — planned or otherwise.

What triggers an exit

Dormant, no revival

A partner that stayed inactive through the full Section 07 escalation path (Day 0–90+) with no reactivation agreed at Leadership review.

Breach of terms

Exclusivity violated, quality standards ignored on a white-label build, or confidentiality broken — reviewed by Leadership, exit can be immediate.

Strategic mismatch

The partnership no longer serves a category Vinsinfo is prioritising — a planned, mutual wind-down rather than a fault-based exit.

Partner-initiated

The partner chooses to leave. Same checklist applies regardless of which side initiates.

The offboarding checklist

1
Notice & sign-off

Standard notice period per the signed agreement (30 days unless otherwise stated); Leadership confirms the exit before anything is communicated to the partner.

2
Client transition plan

Any live client work is handed to a named owner with a documented handover — no client is left without a clear point of contact mid-engagement.

3
Deregister everywhere

Remove the partner's active status from CRM, revoke any live platform-partner ID links (Engine A), and close shared credentials or access.

4
Confidentiality tail

NDA and data-handling obligations from the original agreement continue to apply after exit — confirm this in the closing correspondence.

5
Archive, don't delete

The full partner record stays in CRM as inactive/archived — commission history and QBR notes are kept for audit, not erased.

Re-engagement later

An archived partner isn't blacklisted. If circumstances change, they re-enter through the normal Section 06 scoring process like any new candidate — past history is visible in their archived record, not held against them by default.

10 · For delivery teams

Tools & integrations — the decision flow

Every build defaults to Vinsinfo's existing partnered ecosystem — the SaaS, cloud and ecosystem tools already covered in Section 03. Delivery teams do not integrate a new, unpartnered tool on their own judgement, even for a small feature. If a client specifically asks for something outside our partnered list, this is the flow to follow instead of saying yes or no on the spot.

Other things delivery teams should know

Always check the client's build against the Section 03 category list before quoting integration work — don't assume.

Delivery teams do not negotiate commercial terms with any vendor or partner directly — that sits with the Partnerships Team and Leadership.

If a partner tool is out of date, deprecated, or underperforming on a live project, flag it to the Partnerships Team rather than quietly substituting something else.

When in doubt about which category, model, or partner applies, ask the Partnerships Team before quoting the client anything.

11 · Growing the network

Sourcing new partners, on purpose

Partnerships don't show up on their own — someone has to go find them. This feeds both Engine A and Engine B with new partners to run through the framework above.

Why sourcing has to be deliberate, not an afterthought

Platform commission only exists once a customer is registered under our partner ID — no tagged customer, no payout, however good the build is. Getting that customer in the first place often means going through an agency, consultant, or specialist who trusts us enough to send work our way. In other words: we need agency and channel partners to generate the leads that let our platform partnerships (Engine A) pay out at all. Waiting for the right partner to appear isn't a strategy — sourcing has to run continuously, in parallel with everything else in this playbook.

The four-step procedure

1
Identify the gap

Leadership names the category short on partners this quarter — e.g. "we need agency partners who can send us Shopify or AWS leads" — before anyone starts searching.

2
Search the right channels

Use the channel grid below — don't rely on one source. Log every candidate found, even ones not pursued yet, so the pipeline is visible.

3
Shortlist & qualify

Run every serious candidate through the Section 06 scorer before a conversation goes further than an intro call. Score below "Pilot first" gets parked, not pursued.

4
First conversation & next steps

Use the talking-point checklist below on the intro call, then route the outcome into the Section 07 onboarding steps if it's a go.

Where to search

Platform partner directories

Platforms like AWS, Shopify and Google Cloud publish public directories of their own registered agencies — the exact people already set up to send us tagged customers.

e.g. AWS Partner Finder, Shopify Partner & Experts directory
Industry events & communities

Conferences, meetups, and niche Slack or Discord communities for a platform's ecosystem are where its agency partners already gather to talk shop.

e.g. platform-run partner summits, regional e-commerce meetups
Warm referrals

Existing clients and partners usually know an agency or specialist who'd be a fit — this route converts fastest because trust is already borrowed.

e.g. ask at the 90-day check-in, or the quarterly sync
Agency marketplaces

Listing sites let you filter by specialism and region, and show enough public work history to pre-screen before ever reaching out.

e.g. Clutch, GoodFirms, Upwork Agency listings

What to cover on the first call

01

What kind of clients do they actually bring — size, industry, region — and does that match what we said we needed in Step 1?

02

Can they name a real, current client they'd send our way, or is this a hypothetical relationship?

03

Which of the Section 04 models fits how they want to work — referral, white-label, agency partnership, staff aug, or implementation support?

04

Who's their one named point of contact, and who's ours — no relationship starts without both.

05

Set clear next steps before hanging up: score the partner, decide go / pilot / pass, and confirm who signs the agreement.

What happens next

A scored, qualified candidate moves straight into the Section 07 onboarding steps — pick the model, sign the agreement, register in CRM. Anyone who doesn't clear the scorer stays logged as a prospect and gets revisited next quarter, not chased indefinitely.

12 · Guardrails

Conflict rules & watch-outs

Rules for when two parts of the business could reasonably claim the same partner or the same client.

Attribution is CRM-logged, not assumed

Who introduced whom, and when, is decided from the CRM record made at the time — not from memory, and not automatically to whoever happened to speak first.

Exclusivity must be explicit

A partner isn't assumed exclusive to Vinsinfo, and we aren't assumed exclusive to them, unless the signed agreement says so in writing.

One delivery owner

Every engagement has exactly one accountable Vinsinfo lead.

No cross-model poaching

A referred client can't be quietly re-approached for white-label without sign-off.

Engine A + B can co-exist

A referred client eligible for platform commission earns both — they're not exclusive.

Unregistered platform work

Building before registering the client — the #1 way commission is lost.

Per-deal rate negotiation

Undermines the card and creates resentment between partners.

Silent scope creep

Delivering beyond a signed SOW without a change order.

Quality slipping on white-label

Invisible to the client ≠ lower QA bar. Reputational risk stays with us.

Leadership confirmation is mandatory

Kindly note: every partnership establishment — and every finance-related term inside it, including commission percentages, rate-card exceptions, and payout schedules — requires explicit Leadership sign-off before it is treated as active. No partnership is binding on Vinsinfo without it.

This playbook applies to every Line of Business, without exception

Sales, Marketing, Delivery, and Partnerships all operate under this one operating model — there is no LOB-specific variant, side agreement, or legacy process. 100% adoption is the standard this playbook is published against, tracked by the Partnerships Owner alongside monthly reporting.
